## 5. Verdict: 720p / 8 s on an RTX 2060 6 GB in 30-40 minutes (ESTIMATE)

* **nivra_medium** (fp16): 20 steps: 15-28 min, 30 steps: 20-38 min, 40 steps: 25-47 min, 50 steps: 31-57 min.
  Within 40 minutes the sampler can afford about 32 CFG steps under the pessimistic assumptions and about 66 under the optimistic ones (steps that fit in the table: optimistic [20, 30, 40, 50], pessimistic [20, 30]).
  Plain answer: **plausible but not demonstrated.** With fp16 weights resident (no int8 needed: 1.74 GiB weights + 1.53 GiB activations fit in 6 GB), the 2nd-order `ab2` sampler at about 20-30 CFG steps lands in or near 30-40 minutes if Turing sustains the assumed throughput; 40+ steps only fits under the optimistic assumptions. Attention over 28.5 k tokens is 73 % of the FLOPs and runs on the memory-efficient SDPA kernel, so the attention throughput assumption dominates the uncertainty. int8 weights are not needed for memory and would cost time (dequantisation); they are the fallback for 4-5 GB cards.
* **nivra_large** (int8): 20 steps: 28-52 min, 30 steps: 41-73 min, 40 steps: 53-94 min, 50 steps: 66-115 min.
  Large does not fit in 6 GB with fp16 weights; it needs int8 weights (fits) or block offload, and at ~3x Medium's FLOPs per step it is not a 30-40 minute model on a 2060.

## 6. Untested / caveats

* All CUDA paths (fp16 autocast and `.half()` inference, GradScaler, pinned-memory/stream prefetch offload, cuDNN conv3d, SDPA kernel choice on sm_75) are implemented but untested on GPU.
* Decode tiling with a margin smaller than the decoder's receptive radius is approximate (blended seams); the configured margin is in section 1. Exactness at margin = radius is covered by tests.
* Activation and peak-memory figures are model estimates, not allocator measurements.
